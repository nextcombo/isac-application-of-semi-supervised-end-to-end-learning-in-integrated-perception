# ISAC: Application of Semi-Supervised End-to-End Learning in Integrated Perception and Communications [with MATLAB Code]

Integrated Sensor and Communications (ISAC) is considered one of the key enabling technologies for next-generation wireless systems, enhancing hardware, spectrum, and energy efficiency. This paper investigates an ISAC transceiver with a damaged uniform linear array, which performs single target detection and position estimation as well as multi-input single-output communication. A differentiable model-based learning approach was adopted to optimize both the transmitter and the sensor receiver in an end-to-end manner. An unsupervised loss function that achieves damage compensation without labeled data was proposed. Additionally, a semi-supervised learning strategy was introduced, combining a small amount of labeled data with unlabeled data. The results show that semi-supervised learning can achieve similar performance to supervised learning, with a reduction of 98.8% required labeled data.
Introduction Integrated Sensor and Communications (ISAC) refers to the integration of perception and communication resources to improve system efficiency or performance, or to add new functionalities to the system. In ISAC systems, communication and perception tasks share the same spectrum, hardware, or signal processing algorithms, thereby enhancing hardware, spectrum, or energy efficiency [1]. Emerging technologies such as digital twins, active recognition, and extended reality are largely driven by ISAC, making it one of the key enabling technologies for next-generation wireless systems.
ISAC design largely relies on model-based signal processing algorithms, which provide performance guarantees, interpretability, and predictable computational complexity [5]-[9]. However, in the event of unexpected hardware damage such as antenna distortion, sampling jitter, or calibration errors [10]-[12], the performance of model-based algorithms can significantly degrade. With the advent of deep neural networks (DNNs), deep learning (DL) has recently been used to address hardware damage and model mismatch issues, categorizing into two types in ISAC: (i) single-component deep learning, which optimizes a DNN for performing either transmission or reception operations independently [13]-[15]; (ii) end-to-end deep learning, where both tasks are implemented as DNNs forming an autoencoder (AE) architecture and jointly optimized [16], [17], [18]. DNNs are essentially black boxes, limiting the interpretability of the learned functions. Moreover, they typically require large amounts of labeled training data.
The limited interpretability of DNNs can be overcome by adhering to the model-based machine learning (MB-ML) paradigm [19], which involves using existing signal processing models to initialize, construct, and train learning methods. Additionally, since MB-ML relies on specific architectures for a given problem, it typically requires less labeled data compared to DNNs that are model-agnostic. Recently, MB-ML has been applied in multiple communication [20]-[22], perception [23], [24], and ISAC [25] scenarios. Despite this, previous MB-ML methods [20]-[25] relied on labeled data for training MB-ML models, whereas in the context of perception, especially in autonomous vehicles, obtaining labeled data may be difficult or time-consuming. The demand for labeled data can be further reduced through semi-supervised learning (SSL) or unsupervised learning (UL). UL learns solely from observed (unlabeled) data, while SSL combines limited labeled data with unlabeled data [26]. The integration of MB-ML and UL methods has been applied in the field of channel estimation in communication systems [27], [28]. However, the use of unlabeled data in MB-ML for ISAC has not yet been explored. This topic deserves special attention, as obtaining real positions of all targets in a scene via external sensors (such as cameras or GNSS) in the perception environment could be challenging.
This paper proposes a new method for implementing UL and SSL in the ISAC scenario. The study considers the single-baseline multi-input multi-output (MIMO) radar performing single target estimation and the multi-input single-output (MISO) communication link, as shown in Figure 1. This is a special case of the multi-target estimation scenario recently studied in [25]. Compared to [25] and other end-to-end learning works on ISAC [17], [18], the main innovation of this paper is the use of unlabeled data to reduce the acquisition of labeled data while maintaining similar performance to the fully supervised case. A corresponding unsupervised loss function is proposed and thoroughly investigated, comparing: (i) models without learning; (ii) supervised learning (SL); (iii) UL; and (iv) SSL with varying degrees of labeled data.

Conclusion In this work, the study investigates unsupervised and semi-supervised learning in ISAC under antenna inter-spacing damage. An unsupervised loss function considering damage is proposed. The results show that relying solely on the proposed UL cannot achieve similar performance to SL. When available labeled data samples are limited, finite-data SL will decrease in both false detection probability and symbol error rate. However, SSL starting from limited data SL shows similar performance to perfect damage knowledge and infinite data SL. The current work is limited to single-target perception, future work will consider UL for multiple targets.
